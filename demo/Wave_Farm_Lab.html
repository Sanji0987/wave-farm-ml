About this model

For each buoy it reads the neighbourhood in the wave's frame: the 12 nearest buoys, how many sit up-wave and down-wave within 150 to 1,200 m, and the gap to the nearest buoy in front, behind and beside. 300 boosted trees turn that into the buoy's power; farm power is the sum of the 49 buoys.

Trained on the 10,218 unique Perth layouts except 15% of layout families, held back so Unseen A to D are fair tests. In 5-fold cross-validation with families grouped: .

The best sites for this buoy are not the ones with the most wave power

    Why: this buoy is tuned to short waves

    Resonance is near 7.6 s. Perth has 50.8 kW/m of waves against Sydney's 15.3, yet one buoy absorbs about 9% less at Perth (96.4 vs 105.8 kW).

    Energy is absorbed power from a linear model, so read it as an upper bound: at these sites the buoy captures 60 to 75% of the wave power across its own width, where typical heaving devices manage 16 ± 10% (Babarit 2015). With ERA5 peak period in place of mean period, site 1 gives about 21 GWh/yr and ranks 16th.

    Data: UCI Large-scale Wave Energy Farm dataset (Perth, 49 buoys; 36,043 rows), ERA5 reanalysis 2016–17, GEBCO 2026 bathymetry, and the WEC array simulator calibrated in this project. All power figures are absorbed power.
